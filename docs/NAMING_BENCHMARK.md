# What the names that won actually look like — 20 global, 10 Thai

Compiled 2026-09-24, asked for while choosing our own name. The question was not "what are they
called" but **what the successful names have in common, and what a name does to the image of the
company and the service behind it** — restricted to the two shapes we are: **Service Model** (the
customer uses the app) and **System Model** (the customer builds on top of it).

This is a study document. It is here because the choice it informs is expensive to reverse —
see §5, which is the whole reason this was written.

---

## 1. Twenty global — System Model

The customer is a developer or an IT department. The name has to sound **reliable**, not likeable.

| Name | Letters | Origin | What the name does to the image |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stripe** | 6 | The magnetic stripe on a card. Was `/dev/payments` before | A physical object, borrowed. Sounds like a component, not a service — which is exactly the position: infrastructure you slot in |
| **Twilio** | 6 | Coined | Means nothing, so it cannot be misread. Its only job is to be unmistakable in a documentation URL |
| **Okta** | 4 | **A meteorological unit** — one eighth of sky covered by cloud | Quietly perfect for a cloud identity company, and invisible to anyone who does not know. The story rewards the curious without depending on them |
| **Snowflake** | 9 | No two are alike | A warm image on a cold product. Long for this category and it works anyway, because the image is concrete |
| **Datadog** | 7 | Coined, canine | Deliberately unserious in a category of grim names. "The dog watches your data" needs no explaining |
| **Cloudflare** | 10 | cloud + flare | Descriptive, and the longest here. It says the category out loud — a decision with a ceiling attached |
| **MongoDB** | 7 | From **hu-mongo-us** | The name is a joke about scale. Carries a `DB` suffix that permanently fixes what it is |
| **Vercel** | 6 | "versatile cell". Formerly **ZEIT** | Renamed to escape a name that was German, abstract and hard to spell |
| **Plaid** | 5 | A fabric pattern | Arbitrary, ordinary, memorable. Nothing to do with banking, which is why it can own it |
| **Segment** | 7 | A real word from its own domain (customer segments) | Understandable instantly, and unsearchable forever. The cost of a common word |

## 2. Twenty global — Service Model

The customer is a person doing their job. The names go **warm and verb-like**.

| Name | Letters | Origin | What the name does to the image |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Slack** | 5 | Backronym: *Searchable Log of All Conversation and Knowledge* | Chose a word meaning "slow, loose, idle" for a work tool. The irony is the personality |
| **Zoom** | 4 | The sound of speed | One syllable, a verb, an onomatopoeia. Became a verb in general use — the ceiling of what a name can achieve |
| **Figma** | 5 | From *figure* | Coined, short, soft. Sounds like a craft tool, not enterprise software |
| **Notion** | 6 | A real word: an idea, a thought | Abstract enough to have grown from notes → database → wiki → AI without ever fighting its own name |
| **Canva** | 5 | *canvas*, one letter removed | The most efficient name here: keeps the meaning, sheds the generic word, becomes ownable |
| **Shopify** | 7 | shop + -ify. Formerly **Snowdevil** | Descriptive and proud of it. Everyone knows what it does; it can never be anything else |
| **Salesforce** | 10 | Literally a sales force | **The cautionary tale.** Permanently "the CRM company" — it had to invent *Customer 360* and buy Slack and Tableau to argue it is more |
| **Asana** | 5 | Sanskrit **आसन** — a yoga posture | Borrowed calm. A task manager named after stillness is a positioning statement |
| **Miro** | 4 | The painter **Joan Miró**. Formerly *RealtimeBoard* | Renamed from a description to a person. Four letters replaced twelve |
| **Gusto** | 5 | Relish, enthusiasm. Formerly **ZenPayroll** | Renamed to escape the word *payroll* once it sold benefits and HR too |

## 3. Ten Thai

| Name | Model | Origin | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Omise** | System | Japanese **お店** = shop | See §5. Renamed to Opn, then renamed back |
| **2C2P** | System | *Cash and Card Payment Processor* | Unsayable on a phone call, unsearchable, and it describes only the 2003 business. Grew anyway — because in payments the buyer is an integrator reading a contract, not a shopper |
| **Amity** *(Eko)* | System | Latin *amicitia* — friendship | **A parent brand created to hold several products.** Eko stayed a product; Amity became the company. Closest structure to ours |
| **Bitkub** | Service | **bit + ครับ** | The best Thai wordplay in this list. Instantly warm to a Thai, completely meaningless outside Thailand |
| **Horganice** | Service | **หอ + organize + nice** | Three-way pun for dormitory management. Beloved locally, unexportable, and hard to spell after hearing |

| **Wongnai** | Service | **วงใน** — the inside circle | A real Thai word, and the exact feeling of the product. Now *LINE MAN Wongnai* — a strong local name kept, with a global one bolted in front |

## 4. What they have in common

Counted across the twenty global names:

| | |
|---|---|
| **7 letters or fewer** | **17 of 20** *(only Snowflake, Cloudflare, Salesforce are longer)* |
| **One or two syllables** | **14 of 20** |
| **One obvious pronunciation on first sight** | **20 of 20** |
| **Says what the company does** | **4 of 20** — Salesforce, Shopify, Cloudflare, Segment |

**Three quarters of the winning names tell you nothing about the product.** That is not an accident,
and it is the single clearest pattern in the data.

### The ceiling that descriptive names build

Every descriptive name in both tables is carrying a cost it cannot put down:

- **Salesforce** buys Slack and Tableau and invents *Customer 360*, and is still the CRM company
- **Gusto** had to stop being *ZenPayroll* to sell benefits
- **Miro** had to stop being *RealtimeBoard* to be more than a whiteboard
- **Vercel** had to stop being *ZEIT*
- **Shopify** is excellent at being Shopify and can never be anything else
- **FlowAccount**, **PEAK**, **QueQ**, **2C2P** — each named for the product they had on day one

> **A descriptive name is a rebrand scheduled for the year you succeed** — payable at the worst
> possible moment, when the old name finally has equity worth losing.

### Service names are warm; System names are firm

The split the question asked about is real:

- **System / infrastructure:** Stripe · Okta · Plaid · Segment · Vercel · Twilio — **objects and coinages.** Cold, precise, unsentimental. Nobody wants a *cute* database
- **Service / end-user:** Slack · Zoom · Notion · Canva · Gusto · Asana — **verbs and feelings.** They have a personality because a person has to want to open them

**Ingredient brands sit on the System side without exception.** *Intel Inside* · *Powered by Stripe* ·
*Powered by Shopify* — every one is short and firm. **Not one is soft.**

## 5. 🔴 The Omise case, which is why this document exists

| | |
|---|---|
| 2013 | Founded as **Omise** — Japanese お店, "shop" |
| May 2022 | Parent SYNQA rebrands to **Opn**, alongside a $120M Series C+. Stated reason: payments were no longer the whole business, and the company wanted a **borderless, global** identity |
| Aug 2022 | The gateway itself is renamed **Opn Payments**. New visual identity |
| **Mar 2025** | **Renamed back to Omise.** Stated reason: *its legacy of trust and its reputation across Thailand, Japan and the USA* |

Two years, two rebrands, and back where it started. The newer name was shorter, more global and more
abstract — **everything this document says a good name should be — and it lost to the name people
already trusted.**

And a detail worth more than the announcement: **`api.omise.co` was never changed.** Through both
rebrands the technical surface stayed Omise. A rename is always shallower than it looks, and the
parts that do not move are the parts customers depend on.

### The three lessons, stated plainly

1. **Brand equity lives in the old name. A rename spends it** — it does not transfer it.
2. **"Better" loses to "trusted".** Once a name has years behind it, its objective qualities stop mattering.
3. **Therefore the only cheap time to choose is before there is equity** — which is now, with one
   school live and no market position to protect. In three years this decision costs what it cost Omise.

## 6. What this means for our own choice

### Our architecture is Amity's, not Shopify's

Each school puts **its own name** on the app. Ours appears in one small line: **ขับเคลื่อนโดย ___**.
That is the Amity structure — a parent brand holding products — and the Intel Inside position. So the
name is judged in the **System** slot even though teachers and parents use the software daily.

### Measured against the benchmark, our finalists are all long

| | Letters | Syllables | vs. the 20 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TONKLA** | 6 | **2** | Inside the norm on both |
| **FILIZA** | 6 | 3 | Letters fine, a syllable over |
| **ATOVIA** | 6 | 3–4 | Letters fine, longest to say |
| **TRAKIVO** | 7 | 3 | At the limit on both |
| **ATRAVIK** | 7 | 3 | At the limit on both |

**TONKLA is the only finalist that sits inside the benchmark on both measures** — and it is also the
only one a Thai understands without being told, which is the Bitkub/Wongnai strength, with the
Bitkub/Wongnai export cost attached.

### The unresolved question this study exposes

**FILIZA is warmer than every ingredient brand in the benchmark.** Not one of Stripe, Okta, Plaid,
Segment or Intel is soft. That warmth is an asset with the parent reading a report on her phone and
a question mark with the ผอ. signing a three-year contract.

> **Which audience is the name for — the parent, or the director who signs?**
> They pull opposite ways, and the benchmark cannot answer it. That one is the owner's.
